# ORM COORDINATION GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES

## Purpose

This artifact formalizes ORM's operating principles for ecosystem coordination, relationship escalation, neutrality protection, commercialization governance, and deal-protection discipline.

It exists to answer emerging operating questions raised by the ecosystem-architecture and partner-topology work without prematurely hardening legal, regulatory, brokerage, securities, compensation, or commercialization assumptions into doctrine.

This artifact does not:

- create legal obligations
- create compensation terms
- define securities structures
- create public claims
- finalize commercialization doctrine

It is an internal governance artifact for coordination discipline and escalation logic.

---

## Current Governance Model

The current model remains:

- `ORM` governs and coordinates
- `CEH` standardizes
- `DGE&I` executes

Additional operating rules remain in force:

- multiple execution topologies may eventually exist
- CEH neutrality must be preserved
- ecosystem coordination is distinct from ownership or control
- partner mentions remain internal unless separately approved for outward use
- repo-local doctrine overrides conversational memory and ungoverned interpretation

This model means ORM may play a meaningful institutional role even where it does not own all underlying assets, contracts, or execution entities directly.

---

## Core Coordination Principle

ORM's strategic role is ecosystem coordination, translation, governance continuity, commercialization facilitation, and standards stewardship across converging infrastructure systems.

Those systems may include:

- compute
- energy
- infrastructure
- capital
- standards
- risk
- procurement
- market access
- deployment

ORM's leverage is therefore not necessarily dependent on owning every underlying asset or controlling every execution vehicle. Its value may instead arise from:

- governing how ecosystem parts relate to one another
- translating between technical, commercial, and standards domains
- preserving neutrality where standards credibility matters
- facilitating structured commercialization without collapsing hierarchy
- carrying continuity across multiple parties and multiple opportunity stages

## Relationship-State Governance

The following relationship stages should be used as operational classifications:

- `exploratory conversation`
- `internal reference relationship`
- `structured introduction`
- `NDA stage`
- `protected coordination stage`
- `advisory / engagement stage`
- `commercialization exploration`
- `JV / strategic structure exploration`
- `approved outward reference`
- `public relationship approval`

These stages are operational classifications, not legal conclusions.

Their purpose is to help ORM distinguish between:

- early contact
- meaningful but unpapered coordination
- protected information exchange
- active commercialization discussion
- externally usable relationship language

The stages should not be confused with:

- formal agency
- commitment authority
- fee entitlement
- approved public claim status

---

## Escalation Principles

ORM should escalate documentation, review, and governance controls when one or more of the following conditions appears:

- introductions may create compensable value
- multiple parties become involved
- standards or IP concepts are shared
- commercialization pathways emerge
- deployment coordination emerges
- referral or brokerage ambiguity appears
- ecosystem participants could bypass ORM
- CEH neutrality could be impaired
- public claims become possible

The underlying principle is simple:

informal coordination may be appropriate early, but it should not remain informal once value concentration, role ambiguity, or claim exposure increases materially.

## CEH Neutrality Protection

CEH should remain governance-controlled under ORM.

Accordingly:

- CEH should not appear captured by one execution topology
- commercialization relationships should not undermine standards credibility
- public positioning should preserve standards legitimacy
- execution participants may reference CEH without controlling CEH
- CEH-related commercial activity should be reviewed for neutrality implications before outward use

This is especially important where:

- DGE&I is involved in commercialization pathways
- capital relationships may prefer exclusivity or privileged claims
- benchmarking, certification, or licensing concepts begin to form

If CEH appears structurally subordinate to one execution or commercialization participant, standards credibility may erode even if no formal governance change has occurred.

---

## Multi-Party Opportunity Governance

When multiple ecosystem participants are involved:

- ORM should preserve clarity around its coordination role
- introductions should be documented appropriately
- role ambiguity should be reduced early
- public claims should remain restricted
- neutrality and hierarchy should remain protected
- commercialization assumptions should not remain implicit indefinitely

Multi-party opportunities are often where the largest risks appear:

- bypass risk
- duplicated claims
- unclear fee expectations
- blurred standards boundaries
- premature public naming

ORM should therefore treat multi-party coordination as a governance condition, not merely a business-development convenience.

---

## Public-Reference Governance

Partner references should distinguish among:

- exploratory conversation
- strategic discussion
- advisory relationship
- execution participation
- standards participation
- commercialization exploration
- approved outward reference

Partner consent and legal or business review may be required before outward references are used.

The governance rule is that internal shorthand should not become public language by default.

Public-reference discipline matters because:

- a name can imply endorsement
- a discussion can be mistaken for a commitment
- a standards participant can be mistaken for a controller
- a coordination relationship can be mistaken for ownership or exclusivity

## Legal Review Thresholds

Candidate legal-review triggers include:

- referral or brokerage economics
- licensing structures
- JV structures
- standards participation agreements
- exclusivity
- commercialization frameworks
- CEH or IP usage
- public claims
- equity participation
- multi-party compensation structures
- securities or regulatory adjacency

These thresholds are governance guidance, not final legal policy.

Their purpose is to identify when ORM should stop relying on informal interpretation and move toward formal review.

## Institutional Risk Areas

Key risk areas include:

- hierarchy collapse
- standards capture
- relationship ambiguity
- public-claim overreach
- commercialization ambiguity
- bypass risk
- over-papering too early
- under-protecting coordination value
- execution partner dominance
- CEH credibility erosion

Some of these risks pull in opposite directions.

For example:

- over-papering too early can chill exploration
- under-protecting too long can destroy coordination value

The governance task is therefore not simply to maximize paperwork. It is to escalate protection and structure at the point where risk, value, and exposure justify it.
