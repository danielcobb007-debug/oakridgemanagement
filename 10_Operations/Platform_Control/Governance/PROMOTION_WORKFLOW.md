# PROMOTION WORKFLOW

## Purpose

This document defines how information is promoted through the ORM governance operating system.

Its purpose is to prevent:

- accidental canonization
- over-promotion of AI output
- ambiguous doctrine hardening into truth
- missing lineage
- unclear approval authority
- supersession confusion

This is the official promotion chain for institutional knowledge.

---

# Core Principle

No artifact should move from conversation or extraction into institutional authority without passing through governed promotion states.

---

# Promotion Chain

The default ORM promotion chain is:

`Evidence`

→ `Extraction`

→ `Normalized Doctrine Candidate`

→ `Clarification Required` when necessary

→ `Approved Doctrine` or `Operational State`

→ `Canonical Governance` when the authority threshold requires it

---

# Stage Definitions

## Stage 1 — Evidence

### Typical Inputs

- raw ChatGPT sessions
- raw Claude sessions
- raw Codex sessions
- screenshots
- notes
- copied exports

### Allowed Actions

- preserve
- classify
- add provenance
- route for extraction

### Not Allowed

- treating evidence as settled doctrine

---

## Stage 2 — Extraction

### Purpose

Convert raw evidence into structured candidate knowledge.

### Typical Outputs

- candidate decisions
- candidate open loops
- candidate strategy signals
- candidate architecture signals
- candidate operational updates

### Allowed Actions

- summarize
- classify
- identify promotion targets
- flag conflicts or uncertainty

### Not Allowed

- promoting directly to canon without review

---

## Stage 3 — Normalized Doctrine Candidate

### Purpose

Create structured institutional interpretation that is reviewable but not yet authoritative.

### Allowed Actions

- normalize recurring patterns
- reconcile multiple source artifacts
- assign confidence and status
- identify doctrinal implications
- propose canonical or operational destinations

### Required Metadata

- source lineage
- confidence
- status
- classification
- promotion target
- clarification need
- supersedes / superseded by

### Not Allowed

- being treated as settled doctrine without review

---

## Stage 4 — Clarification Required

### Purpose

Hold normalized items that require executive, legal, governance, or strategic clarification before promotion.

### Use When

- ownership is unresolved
- monetization model is unresolved
- authority boundaries are unclear
- multiple interpretations remain plausible
- promotion would create institutional risk

### Allowed Actions

- preserve ambiguity explicitly
- route questions into open loops
- await decision

### Not Allowed

- forcing ambiguous items into doctrine just to simplify the system

---

## Stage 5 — Approved Doctrine Or Operational State

### Approved Doctrine

Use when the item defines:

- durable rules
- architecture boundaries
- standards boundaries
- AI operating rules
- deployment or audit rules

### Operational State

Use when the item defines:

- current mappings
- active workstreams
- current deployments
- live operating conditions
- current unresolved operational reality

### Approval Rule

Promotion should occur only when:

- lineage is sufficient
- ambiguity is resolved enough
- the target layer is clear
- governance compatibility is confirmed

---

## Stage 6 — Canonical Governance

### Purpose

Reserved for constitutional changes and highest-authority institutional truth.

### Use When

The promoted item changes:

- system-of-record authority
- entity boundaries
- domain authority
- repo authority
- ownership hierarchy

### Approval Rule

Canonical promotion requires explicit approval and should remain rare.

# Promotion Triggers

Promotion should be considered when an artifact has meaningful:

- governance impact
- architectural impact
- operational impact
- standards impact
- capital-allocation impact
- workflow-standardization impact

If none of those thresholds are met, the artifact may remain as evidence, memory, or exploration.

---

# Supersession Handling

When promoting new doctrine or state:

1. identify what is being replaced, if anything
2. mark superseded artifacts clearly where practical
3. preserve lineage
4. avoid silent replacement of doctrine

Supersession rule:

New promotion does not erase historical context.
It establishes current authority.

---

# Rollback Rules

If a promoted item is later found to be incorrect:

1. stop downstream propagation
2. identify affected doctrine, state, or workflows
3. restore prior governed understanding if needed
4. document the correction
5. preserve the supersession trail

Rollback should preserve institutional traceability rather than hide the mistake.

---

# Observability Requirements

Every meaningful promotion should make it possible to answer:

- what artifact was promoted
- from which source lineage
- into which target layer
- by what authority
- with what confidence
- whether clarification was required
- what it superseded
- what downstream artifacts were affected

If these questions cannot be answered, promotion observability is insufficient.

---

# Failure Modes To Avoid

- evidence treated as canon
- extraction treated as doctrine
- normalized candidates treated as approved truth
- unresolved ambiguity forced into settled categories
- silent supersession
- implementation redefining governance

---

# Non-Negotiable Principle

Promotion is a governance act, not a formatting step.

Institutional knowledge becomes stable only when promotion is intentional, observable, and reversible.
