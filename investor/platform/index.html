125% Generation Architecture
▸ Design Principle

Oversized by Design

Every DGE&I facility is sized to generate 125% of maximum rated nameplate capacity (4 Power Blocks + N+1). This is not backup headroom — it is a deliberate revenue architecture.

AI inference and training workloads are inherently bursty. Peak GPU utilization across a fleet rarely sustains 100% for extended periods. The delta between actual consumption and generation capacity is the monetizable surplus.

The EMS tracks utilization in real time and routes every unused kilowatt-hour to the utility tie automatically, while maintaining full N+1+1 redundancy for the compute load.

▸ Revenue Flow

Power as Profit Center

Traditional data center economics treat power as a fixed operating cost. The DGE&I 125% architecture inverts this model.

▸ Bloom SOFC generates 125% of rated nameplate
▸ Compute load consumes ~70–85% at typical utilization
▸ EMS identifies surplus in real time · preserves N+1 reserve
▸ Surplus exported to utility tie via DC-AC inversion

Architecture Deep Dive — Core DC vs Edge DC

The real distinction between Core DC and Edge DC is not only rack density — it is scalability ceiling and ecosystem alignment.

▸ SKU 02 · Practical DC-Native

Core DC

Standard DC-to-DC · ~400 VDC
+ Step-up from AC — still requires some custom DC-rated gear (breakers, busway, protection) but is not tied to NVIDIA's exact 800 V reference spec.
+ Efficiency gain comes primarily from eliminating the Bloom skid AC conversion stage and rack-level rectification.
+ kW per rack headroom improves meaningfully over REV1 AC due to reduced waste heat — but not as dramatically as 800 V.
+ Good for customers who want DC efficiency today without committing to NVIDIA's 800 V reference design across the full facility.
Core DC = better efficiency + moderate density uplift with "good enough" DC gear.
▸ SKU 03 · Flagship Architecture

Edge DC

NVIDIA-Native 800 VDC · Bloom-Native
+ Flagship because Bloom fuel cells already output ~800 V DC natively, and NVIDIA designed the entire GB300 / Blackwell / Kyber / Rubin ecosystem around this voltage.
+ 800 V slashes current dramatically → far less copper, smaller busway, lower I²R losses. Enables sustained rack densities beyond the current 250 kW CDU limit (NVIDIA roadmap: 1 MW+ racks by 2027).
+ Fewest conversion stages overall — direct 800 V feed to rack PDUs / DC-DC converters in SuperMicro B300 and successor clusters.
+ Simplifies N+1 export / sell-back: DC-AC inversion occurs only at the utility tie point, not distributed across the compute floor.
Edge DC = maximum allowable kW/rack + future-proof because it is the exact architecture NVIDIA, Bloom, TI, Delta, Eaton, and the broader ecosystem are building around.
