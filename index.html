Standards Origination

CEH™ — The unit AI infrastructure was missing.

Compute Energy Hour. The first standard unit to bridge hardware performance, energy economics, and carbon accounting into a single auditable number. Originated by Oak Ridge Management, April 2026.

Formal Definition

CEH™ is defined as kilowatt-hours of facility energy consumed per unit of compute output per hour, adjusted for hardware utilization and facility overhead.

CEH™ = ( TDP_W × N × u × PUE ) ÷ 1,000 ÷ COU/hr CEH™ Cost = CEH™ × $/kWh CEH™ Carbon = CEH™ × kg CO₂/kWh

Energy-agnostic. Workload-portable. Reproducible by any party from disclosed inputs. Lower CEH™ = greater energy efficiency per unit of useful compute output.

Daniel Cobb is the Founder of Oak Ridge Management, a platform built to operate at the intersection of infrastructure, capital, and applied intelligence.

Cobb's career has evolved from advising on risk to architecting platforms — bringing together capital partners, operators, and demand into cohesive, scalable systems. His work spans large-scale infrastructure initiatives, AI hardware deployment, and real asset ownership — including self-storage and mobile home community development — as well as his role as President of Platform Capital & Commercial Strategy for DG Energy & Infrastructure, where he focuses on capital formation, offtake strategy, and integrated platform economics in the data center and energy sectors.

At Oak Ridge, he is building independent intellectual property and market frameworks designed to standardize and unlock value across emerging industries. This includes the development of Compute Energy Hour (CEH™) — a proposed industry benchmark that connects compute output, energy consumption, and cost into a single measurable unit. Alongside CEH™, he holds an early investor and stakeholder position in QumulusAI, acquires and leases GPU server infrastructure to neo-cloud operators, and has established a strategic partnership with VFG Group, which holds a distribution agreement with Inventec — providing supply chain access to hyperscaler-grade server hardware at the distribution layer.

Cobb is known for his ability to connect disparate systems — capital, infrastructure, and demand — and translate them into executable strategies. His approach is grounded in a simple principle: if it can be measured, it can be improved; if it can be structured, it can scale.

Before his career in business, Cobb was a Division I quarterback, playing at both the University of Georgia and Auburn University, where he led teams at the highest levels of college football. His path — including early adversity, transfer, and return to elite competition — shaped the mindset that now defines his work: discipline, accountability, and a relentless focus on execution.

He brings that same philosophy to Oak Ridge Management — building platforms designed not just to participate in markets, but to define them.
